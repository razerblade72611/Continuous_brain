## Meaning of PASS

A PASS supports this functional claim on held-out subjects:

The system can remember discrepancies between expected personal futures and the
personal futures that actually occurred, integrate those discrepancies across a
chronological autobiography, and use that autobiography to form later
commitments.

Changing only autobiographical history changes the later commitment.
Erasing history removes the learned preference.
Preserving A/B labels, chronology, and history length while eliminating
expected-vs-actual discrepancy forces the model to exact neutrality.

Therefore the commitment preference cannot be generated by A/B labels or
chronology alone; expected-vs-actual autobiographical discrepancy is
causally load-bearing by construction.

This is a functional computational result. It is not sufficient evidence of
subjective experience, phenomenal consciousness, sentience, qualia, emotion, or
moral status.
